Click System

The band mixes exactly one click channel. Which click is on it is decided at the A/B switch on stage — this is the whole path, both legs.

The switch is an XLR A/B switch living with the drummer at the Ableton station. Its output goes into the sub-snake on the line labelled CLICK, and that is the only click the band ever sees — everything upstream of the switch is invisible to them.

LegSourcePath
AAbleton clickAbleton → PlayAUDIO OUT 9 → ¼″ TRS breakout down to the rack → XLR back up the snake → switch input A
BResi click (Uplink)Resi feed → console → D2-Rack 2 OUT 11 direct out → snake 2 line 11, reversed with gender benders → switch input B
OUTSelected clickSwitch → sub-snake CLICK line → console → the band's click channel
Leg A — why the loop. OUT 9 is where click is mapped in every Ableton session. The PlayAUDIO's ¼″ TRS breakout runs down to the same point the track feeds enter the D2-Rack — then channel 9 alone turns around and rides back up the snake on its own XLR, so it lands physically at the switch.
Leg B — the gender-bender shenanigans. The sub-snakes have no output lines, so one input line is sacrificed: snake 2 line 11 is reversed with a gender bender at each end. At the rack end, D2-Rack 2 OUT 11 — the Resi click direct out — feeds it; at the stage end, another gender bender and an XLR into switch input B. This is the part that looks wrong when you stare at the patch: it's supposed to look like that.
Snake 2 line 11 corresponds to D2-Rack 1 IN 23 — that's the input given up to make the return.

Diagnose a Symptom

Open the symptom that matches what you're hearing. Each checklist is worked top to bottom in order.

Inputs way down, gain does nothing — no head-amp control on the D2-Racks
SD9 → network → D2-Rack 1 & 2 head amps
  1. Prove it fast with tracks — run tracks from Ableton. They land on the first inputs of the patch, so they're a constant, hands-free test signal. Spin the gain on one of those channels: if the level doesn't move, you've lost head-amp control.
  2. Don't diagnose from the ears — IEM channels sit at unity and barely feel this failure; they keep passing audio. It's the inputs, stuck at whatever gain they were left at (−6 or thereabouts), that give it away.
  3. Don't reach for digital trim — it boosts the signal, but it goes fizzy and weird because there's no real gain underneath it. That fizz is itself confirmation of this diagnosis.
  4. Power everything down — both D2-Racks and the SD9. Wait 10 minutes: the caps have to fully discharge or the stale state survives the restart.
  5. Racks up first — power on both racks and give them at least two minutes to stabilise and find their internal clock.
  6. Console last — power on the SD9. Racks before console, always. Then re-check gain on the Ableton channels.
Why this happens: an abnormal console shutdown leaves the racks looking for an SD9 session that no longer exists on the network, so head-amp control never re-attaches until everything cold-starts.
Lots of popping after a system restart
SuperRack / SoundGrid master clock
  1. It's clocking, not cabling — after a restart SuperRack's master clock defaults to whatever SoundGrid device is up on the network, usually the DiGiGrid MGB. The MGB references the console, which creates a clock loop — the slip is the popping you hear.
  2. Fix it in SuperRack — Setup tab → Inventory → I/O Devices → set Master to the DiGiCo SD I/O (64-channel).
